# muse_moe_w4a16: how to read the kernel

`submission.py` is an exact copy of submit `db79106e9d524b1d` (30 Oct, final batch): **1.8585x**
on the H200, 95% CI [1.831, 1.886], rank 538 of 1203; content_hash `6aafe8b5…`, the same as check
`4796ee91` (1.8627x); sha256 `5c5352b4…`. It is `muse_moe_w4a16/enviados/v55/submission.py` at
`cdf2a65` on `feature/P-2610-okc-moe`. All the compute is mine. The bandwidth context is in [`roofline.md`](roofline.md).

`kernel.py` is the file to read: the same kernels as `submission.py` with dead experiment paths
removed and the docstrings fixed. `submission.py` is the exact file the judge scored (sha256 in
[`evidence/provenance.json`](../../evidence/provenance.json)). Line numbers for every symbol below
are in [`MAP.md`](MAP.md).

What `kernel.py` drops: the sm_75 emulation build (`OKC_EMULATE`), the `PDL_OK` switch, the
`SPLIT_MID` constant (inlined), two Python mirrors of the decode used only by the local tests
(`decode_word`, `scale_pair_fast`) and the import-time version print. It was checked offline after
the judge closed: the same host-side decisions in 345 `kernel_fn` cases and in `_split_k` over
131,072 (bs, K) pairs, identical NVRTC PTX for the filled `CUDA_SRC` (sm_90a), identical Triton
syntax trees, and 16 of 16 shapes on a local RTX 2080 bit-identical to `submission.py`.

**v55 is v24 with two additions:**
- **PDL chain for bs ≤ 16** (`PDL_MAX_BS = 16`, from the bf16_proj work). Each kernel launches its
  successor before it finishes, and the successor waits in `griddepcontrol.wait`.
- **Dynamic per-warp item queue in `gemm()`.** The first item of each warp is fixed. Lane 0 fetches
  the following ones with an `atomicAdd` on `nblk[2]` (gemm1) or `nblk[3]` (gemm2) and broadcasts
  them to the rest of the warp with `__shfl_sync`. Warps that finish early take more work, and the
  last round no longer depends on the slow warps.

This gains +3.8% at bs 128 and +2.6% at bs 256 (+2.6% overall) and improves all six shapes. The
route step (`moe_route` or `_route_prep`) resets the counters on every call, inside the kernel graph, so
it also holds under PDL and under the CUDA graph replay ([`concepts.md`](concepts.md), § 7).

In the full layer the queue does not win. The layer's routing spreads tokens over more experts, and
the static split was already balanced there (decode_layer v54, 1.668x).

The previous mark was v24 (`fc6aaf56`, 1.811x). Everything below describes v24 and still holds for
v55.

**Start with [`concepts.md`](concepts.md).** It is the conceptual document for this kernel, written
for the final version: NVFP4 and bit-level decoding (the subnormal trick, recoded scales, the v8 bit
reordering and the v23 IMAD), `mma.sync` with swap AB and no shared memory, split-K, wgmma and why
the H200 judged it differently from the bench, and warp specialization. Then come back here for the
code.

**What changed from v12 to v24 (+6%).**
- **v23, another bit order (the v8 idea, one more turn).** `CODE_SIGN_BIT` and `CODE_MAG_LOW`
  change so that decode register 2 comes out of one multiplication:
  `((q & 0x40074007) * 66) & 0x81C081C0`. The two shifted copies (`·2` and `·64`) never share a
  bit, so there is no carry and the product equals their OR. 10 operations per word instead of 11.
  It was checked bit by bit. It reads well next to `_imad_form` and
  [`concepts.md`](concepts.md), § 2.4.
- **v20, `int` indices.** Removes all spills from the GEMMs (128 → 126 registers).
- **v24, addressing.** One 64-bit pointer per group and tile: the 16 loads of the group use an
  immediate offset. The scale bias lives in a register (`sbias`, with `ch1 >> 30` as a zero the
  compiler cannot fold), and `(x4 & mask) | sbias` is a single LOP3.

**What changed in v12 (+6% over v8).**
- **v9, recoded scales.** `recode_scales` reorders the 8 bits of each E4M3 scale so that
  `scale_of` extracts the BF16 pair with one `__byte_perm` and one mask instead of ~5 instructions.
  If any byte is not a positive normal, `_scale_bytes` and `_needs_exact` send the whole tensor to
  the exact path. All 256 bytes were checked.
- **v12, item order.** The 4 warps of a CTA work on the same token block with consecutive tiles
  (`b = (item / nwt) % nblk`). Their activations come from L2 once and then hit in L1. It is three
  lines in `gemm()` and gives +9% at verify 16.

**What changed in v8 (+17% over v6, from a single change).** `pack_codes` reorders the **bits**
inside each 32-bit word (`CODE_SIGN_BIT`, `CODE_MAG_LOW`). With that, `decode()` (generated by
`_decode_source()`) builds the four BF16 values of each word with 11 operations (10 since v23)
instead of 15. The inner loop drops from ~3.2 to ~2.7 instructions per value. On the H200, where
the GEMM is bound by instructions and not by memory, this shows; on the bench H100 it barely does.
In addition, `_combine` loads the token rows in a single tile, and `__launch_bounds__(128, 4)` pins
the register count at 128. The decode was verified bit by bit for the 16 codes in the 8
positions. Read with the bit diagram of [`concepts.md`](concepts.md), § 2.3, at hand, it is the
best reading in the repo.

**What v6 (1.367x) already had over v3 (1.220x).**
- `moe_route` in CUDA: one CTA of 1024 threads, with shared-memory atomics and a prefix sum. It
  replaces the Triton `_route_prep` between 64 and 4096 pairs; at bs 256 it goes from 40 µs to
  ~4 µs.
- 4 CTAs/SM in the GEMMs: with 128 registers, 4 fit.
- Split-K 2 also for bs 7-64.
- The Triton path for the GEMMs was pruned.

**What it does.** Each token goes to 8 of the 256 experts; this GPU holds 32. For each (token,
local expert) pair it computes `x @ W13ᵀ` → SwiGLU → `@ W2ᵀ`, normalizes, and adds the result with
its router weight. The weights are NVFP4: 4-bit E2M1 codes, one E4M3 scale per 16 values and one
FP32 scale per expert.

**The module docstring of `kernel.py` explains the whole design and is the best entry point.** In
short:
1. Four steps (plus `_swiglu_reduce` when K is split): `_route_prep` (Triton, ≤ 64 pairs) or `moe_route` (CUDA) group the pairs by expert
   into blocks of ≤ 16 tokens; `moe_gemm1` (CUDA) computes W13 with SwiGLU; `moe_gemm2` (CUDA)
   computes W2 with the scale and `(1 + post_norm_w)`; `_combine` (Triton) does the RMSNorm and the
   weighted sum.
2. **GEMMs without shared memory or barriers.** Each warp is independent and persistent: it takes
   an item (block, columns, K slice), reads the weights with one 16 B load per lane, decodes them
   in registers and calls `mma.sync m16n8k16` with the weights on the M side ("swap AB").
3. **Lossless dequantization with bit arithmetic.** The E2M1 code is placed in a BF16 as a
   subnormal (since v8, with the bits already positioned by `pack_codes`), and a single `bf16x2`
   multiplication by the scale applies the block scale. It is exact because 2 + 4 significant bits
   fit in BF16. It was checked bit by bit.
4. **Bytes reordered in `prepare` (`pack_codes`, `pack_scales`).** Within each 64-wide K chunk,
   each lane's fragment is contiguous and falls in a single scale block. The values do not change;
   only the order of k inside the chunk does, and the sum does not see it.
5. **Split-K by bs range** (≤ 2: 4 slices; ≤ 64: 2; otherwise 1), with FP32 partials and a guard
   that requires the split to divide the 512-wide K groups.

**Reading order.** Everything is in `kernel.py`; line numbers in [`MAP.md`](MAP.md). The CUDA is
also in [`cuda/`](cuda/).
1. The module docstring.
2. `kernel_fn` (at the end): the sequence of launches (four, five with split-K).
3. `CUDA_SRC`, readable as [`cuda/moe_gemm.cu`](cuda/moe_gemm.cu): `decode`, `scale_of`,
   `tile_loop` and `gemm`, in that order.
4. `pack_codes` and `pack_scales`, with the `tile_loop` loop open next to them.
5. `moe_route` (in `CUDA_SRC`), `_route_prep` and `_combine`.

**What is missing for the top 40 (2.47x on 1 Nov, +33%).** The next idea was a tiled
`_combine`. [`what-didnt-win.md`](what-didnt-win.md) lists what was tried and lost on the H200:
wgmma, specialized warps, finer split-K, SwiGLU fusions.
